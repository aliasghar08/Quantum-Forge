Coronary Bifurcation Hemodynamics, Fractional Flow Reserve & PCI Stent Mechanics

Author: Ali Asghar Published: November 13, 2026 Field: Interventional Cardiology & Hemodynamics Curriculum: Cardiology Fellowship / Cath Lab Staff

1. The Fluid Dynamics of Coronary Perfusion

The coronary arterial circulation presents a unique fluid mechanical environment unmatched anywhere else in the human vasculature. While systemic peripheral beds receive peak perfusion during cardiac systole, coronary arterial blood flow occurs predominantly during diastole (70–80% of total volume).

During ventricular systole, powerful myocardial wall contraction generates intramyocardial tissue pressures exceeding intraventricular systolic pressures, physically compressing the microcirculatory capillary bed and driving retrograde or stalled flow in epicardial arteries. In diastole, as the myocardium relaxes and the aortic valve closes, aortic root pressure ($P_a$) drives forward laminar flow across the left main coronary artery (LMCA), left anterior descending (LAD), and circumflex (LCx) branches.

2. Physics of Fractional Flow Reserve (FFR) & iFR

Angiographic visual assessment of coronary stenoses (percent diameter stenosis via 2D fluoroscopy) has notoriously poor correlation with true physiological ischemia. Interventional cardiology relies on invasive pressure-wire physiology:

Fractional Flow Reserve (FFR)

Fractional Flow Reserve represents the fraction of maximum achievable myocardial blood flow in the presence of a stenosis relative to the hypothetical normal vessel. Assuming coronary venous pressure is negligible and microvascular resistance ($R_{\text{myo}}$) is held at a constant, minimal value via pharmacological vasodilation (hyperemia induced by intravenous Adenosine at 140 μg/kg/min):

\text{FFR} = \frac{Q_{\text{stenosis}}^{\max}}{Q_{\text{normal}}^{\max}} = \frac{(P_d - P_v) / R_{\text{myo}}^{\min}}{(P_a - P_v) / R_{\text{myo}}^{\min}} \approx \frac{P_d}{P_a}

Where $P_d$ is mean distal coronary pressure recorded 2–3 cm distal to the lesion by a 0.014-inch optical pressure wire, and $P_a$ is mean proximal aortic pressure recorded at the guide catheter tip during steady-state maximal hyperemia.

  • FFR ≤ 0.80: Hemodynamically significant ischemia; strong clinical trial evidence (FAME I/II, DEFER) supporting revascularization via Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) or Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG).
  • FFR > 0.80: Non-ischemic lesion; revascularization confers no prognostic benefit over optimal medical therapy (OMT), and may provoke iatrogenic stent-related complications.

Instantaneous Wave-Free Period (iFR)

To avoid the bronchospasm, flushing, and transient heart block associated with adenosine infusions, the Instantaneous Wave-Free Period (iFR) samples the pressure ratio $P_d / P_a$ exclusively during a specific 75% temporal window within diastole (from 25% past dicrotic notch to 5 ms before next systole). During this wave-free period, origin waves from the aortic root and reflected microcirculatory waves cease, microvascular resistance naturally drops to its basal nadir, and baseline $P_d/P_a$ matches hyperemic FFR with an ischemic cutoff of iFR ≤ 0.89 (DEFINE-FLAIR, iFR-SWEDEHEART).

3. Bifurcation Anatomy: Branching Scaling Laws

Coronary bifurcation lesions—involving a main vessel (MV) and a side branch (SB)—account for 15–20% of all interventional procedures and carry heightened risk of peri-procedural myocardial infarction and restenosis. The geometry of healthy bifurcations adheres to fundamental biological scaling laws:

  • Murray's Law (Minimum Work Model): Minimizes viscous dissipation and metabolic vascular maintenance:
    r_0^3 = r_1^3 + r_2^3
    Where $r_0$ is proximal main vessel radius, and $r_1, r_2$ are distal main vessel and side branch radii.
  • Finet's Law (Human Coronary Formulation): A validated linear diameter formulation derived from human intravascular ultrasound (IVUS):
    D_0 = 0.678 \cdot (D_1 + D_2)

Endothelial Wall Shear Stress (WSS) & Atherogenesis

Wall Shear Stress ($\tau_w = \mu \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\big|_{y=0}$) is the tangential frictional force exerted by flowing blood on endothelial cells. In bifurcation geometries, high WSS (>70 dyn/cm²) at the flow divider (carina) promotes anti-inflammatory, nitric oxide-mediated endothelial protection.
Conversely, the lateral lateral walls opposite the carina experience flow separation, recirculation vortices, and low/oscillatory shear stress (<10 dyn/cm²), downregulating Kruppel-like factor 2 (KLF2) and triggering atheroma formation and plaque rupture.

4. Comparative Medina Classification & Stenting Strategies

Technique Number of Stents Primary Indication Key Technical Steps & Pitfalls
Provisional Stenting 1 (Stent main vessel; bail-out SB only) True non-complex bifurcations (Medina 1,1,0 or 1,0,1 with SB < 2.5 mm) Gold standard default; requires Proximal Optimization Technique (POT) to appose stent struts at the carina.
Culotte Technique 2 (Full double-layer coverage) Complex bifurcations with similar diameters (MV ≈ SB) Excellent ostial scaffolding, but creates a double metal layer in the proximal main vessel.
T-and-Protrusion (TAP) 2 (Side-branch stent with 1 mm carina overhang) Suboptimal SB result after provisional stenting (T-angle ∼ 90°) Guarantees ostial SB coverage but leaves a small metallic neo-carina.
Double Kissing Crush (DK-Crush) 2 (Side-branch crushed by MV balloon) Complex true bifurcation / Left Main stem (Medina 1,1,1 with long SB lesion > 10 mm) Two separate kissing-balloon inflations (1st post-crush, 2nd post-MV stent). Proven superior in DKCRUSH-V randomized trial.
