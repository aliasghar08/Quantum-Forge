ARDS Respiratory Mechanics: PEEP Recruitment, Driving Pressure, Compliance Loops & V/Q Matching

Author: Ali Asghar Category: Critical Care Pulmonology & Respiratory Mechanics Est. Read Time: 16 min read Updated: November 2026

1. Pathophysiology of ARDS: The Exudative Phase & Alveolar Flooding

Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) is a life-threatening form of acute hypoxemic respiratory failure characterized by non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema, widespread alveolar inflammation, surfactant destruction, and severe ventilation-perfusion (V/Q) mismatching. Under the current Berlin Definition, ARDS is diagnosed by:

  • Onset within 1 week of a known clinical insult or new/worsening respiratory symptoms.
  • Bilateral pulmonary opacities on chest radiography or CT not fully explained by pleural effusions, lobar atelectasis, or lung nodules.
  • Respiratory failure not fully explained by left ventricular heart failure or fluid overload (confirmed via echocardiography or pulmonary artery wedge pressure ≤18 mmHg).
  • Severe hypoxemia measured by the PaO2 / FiO2 (P/F) ratio on a minimum Positive End-Expiratory Pressure (PEEP) ≥5 cmH2O:
    • Mild ARDS: 200 < P/F ≤ 300 mmHg.
    • Moderate ARDS: 100 < P/F ≤ 200 mmHg.
    • Severe ARDS: P/F ≤ 100 mmHg (associated with hospital mortality >45%).

The histological hallmark of ARDS is Diffuse Alveolar Damage (DAD). Pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, IL-6, IL-8) recruit activated neutrophils to the pulmonary microvasculature. Neutrophils release reactive oxygen species (ROS) and elastase, degrading the microvascular endothelium and alveolar epithelial barrier (type I and type II pneumocytes). Protein-rich exudate floods the alveolar spaces, organizing into eosinophilic hyaline membranes that act as physical barriers to gas diffusion.

2. Respiratory Mechanics & Governing Equations

In passive, paralyzed mechanically ventilated patients, total respiratory system mechanics are described by the classical Equation of Motion:

P_aw(t) = P_resistive + P_elastic + PEEP_tot
P_aw(t) = ( Flow · R_aw ) + ( V_T / C_rs ) + PEEP_tot

where:
- P_peak = Peak inspiratory pressure (reflects both airway resistance and lung elastance)
- P_plat = Plateau pressure measured during an end-inspiratory pause (zero flow → purely elastic)
- PEEP_tot = Total PEEP (Extrinsic PEEP + Intrinsic Auto-PEEP)
- R_aw = Airway resistance = ( P_peak - P_plat ) / Inspiratory Flow
- C_rs = Static respiratory system compliance = V_T / ( P_plat - PEEP_tot )

In healthy adults, respiratory system compliance (Crs) is approximately 60 to 100 mL/cmH2O. In severe ARDS, alveolar collapse and fluid filling dramatically shrink functional aerated lung volume—a phenomenon termed the "Baby Lung" (Gattinoni). Static compliance frequently drops below 20 to 30 mL/cmH2O.

3. Driving Pressure (ΔP): The Core Determinant of Ventilator-Induced Lung Injury

Historically, lung-protective ventilation focused exclusively on limiting tidal volume (VT to 6 mL/kg predicted body weight [PBW]) and plateau pressure (Pplat ≤ 30 cmH2O; ARMA trial). However, landmark research by Amato et al. demonstrated that Driving Pressure (ΔP) is the variable most strongly correlated with survival:

ΔP = P_plat - PEEP = V_T / C_rs

Driving pressure represents the tidal volume normalized to the actual compliance of the aerated "baby lung." A tidal volume of 400 mL delivered into a lung with Crs = 50 mL/cmH2O yields a safe ΔP of 8 cmH2O. However, the exact same 400 mL delivered into an ARDS lung with Crs = 20 mL/cmH2O generates a destructive ΔP of 20 cmH2O!

Mechanical Ventilation Metric Safe Protective Target Pathological Threshold VILI Mechanism
Driving Pressure (ΔP) ≤ 14 – 15 cmH2O > 15 cmH2O (Linear increase in mortality) Cyclic mechanical strain causing alveolar rupture and biotrauma.
Plateau Pressure (P_plat) ≤ 28 – 30 cmH2O > 30 cmH2O Barotrauma / Volutrauma: Alveolar overdistension and air leaks (pneumothorax).
Tidal Volume (V_T) 4 – 8 mL/kg PBW > 8–10 mL/kg PBW Volutrauma in compliant non-dependent lung zones.

4. Open-Lung Strategy: PEEP Titration & Alveolar Recruitment

Positive End-Expiratory Pressure (PEEP) prevents end-expiratory alveolar collapse, recruiting unstable dorsal units and improving functional residual capacity (FRC). In the pressure-volume loop, setting PEEP above the Lower Inflection Point (LIP) prevents Atelectrauma—the repetitive opening and closing of derecruited alveoli that shears epithelial walls.

However, excessive PEEP distends already-open alveoli, causing vascular compression of pulmonary capillaries, increasing pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR), acute right ventricular overload (cor pulmonale), and systemic hypotension. An optimal PEEP titration trial identifies the "sweet spot" where compliance is maximized and driving pressure is minimized.

5. Prone Positioning: Fluid Mechanics of Gravity & Pleural Pressure

The PROSEVA trial established that early, prolonged prone positioning (≥16 hours/day) in patients with severe ARDS (P/F < 150 mmHg) cuts 28-day mortality by nearly half (16.0% vs. 32.8%). The mechanism is fundamentally biomechanical:

  • In the Supine Position: The heavy, edematous lung tissue (up to 3x normal weight) and the weight of the heart compress dependent dorsal lung zones. The vertical gradient of pleural pressure (Ppl) is steep (-2 cmH2O anteriorly to +8 cmH2O posteriorly), causing widespread dorsal atelectasis.
  • In the Prone Position: The heart rests on the anterior chest wall, and the cylindrical geometry of the rib cage distributes pleural pressure far more homogeneously throughout the thoracic cavity. Transpulmonary pressure (PL = Paw - Ppl) becomes uniform.
  • V/Q Matching: Pulmonary blood flow remains preferentially distributed to dorsal lung regions due to native vascular branching geometry, regardless of patient orientation. Turning the patient prone re-aerates dorsal alveoli while blood flow remains dorsal, dramatically matching ventilation with perfusion and abolishing intrapulmonary right-to-left shunt.
