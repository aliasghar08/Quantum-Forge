hERG Potassium Channel Blockade, Torsades de Pointes Risk & In-Silico Cardiotoxicity

Author: Ali Asghar Published: November 13, 2026 Field: Cardiac Electrophysiology & Safety Pharmacology Curriculum: Cardiology Fellowship / USMLE Step 1

1. The Ventricular Action Potential & The I_Kr Current

The cardiac ventricular action potential consists of five distinct phases (Phases 0 through 4), orchestrated by the sequential opening and closing of voltage-gated ion channels. Phase 3—the rapid repolarization phase that returns the cardiomyocyte transmembrane potential from its plateau (+20 mV) back to resting baseline (-85 mV)—is driven fundamentally by the outward flow of potassium ions through the rapid delayed rectifier potassium current ($I_{Kr}$).

The pore-forming α-subunit of the $I_{Kr}$ channel is encoded by the human Ether-à-go-go-Related Gene (hERG, also known as KCNH2 or $K_v11.1$). Unintended pharmacological blockade of this single ion channel represents the single most common cause of pre-clinical drug attrition and post-marketing withdrawal in pharmaceutical history (responsible for the market removal of Terfenadine, Cisapride, Astemizole, and Grepafloxacin).

2. Structural Biology of the hERG Cavity (Cryo-EM: PDB 5VA1)

The resolution of the full-length human hERG structure by MacKinnon and colleagues via cryo-electron microscopy (PDB: 5VA1) revealed the precise structural rationale for its notorious promiscuity toward diverse chemical scaffolds:

  • Wide Central Inner Cavity: Unlike typical voltage-gated potassium channels (e.g., Shaker or $K_v1.2$), the central vestibule below the selectivity filter is unusually capacious, accommodating large, lipophilic drug molecules with molecular weights ranging from 300 to over 700 Da.
  • Absence of Proline Kinks: The pore-lining S6 helices lack the canonical Pro-X-Pro motif that constricts other potassium channels, creating a continuous, unconstricted aqueous pocket.
  • Pivotal Aromatic Trapping Residues: Each of the four homotetrameric S6 subunits projects two critical aromatic residues directly into the central pore: Tyrosine-652 (Tyr-652) and Phenylalanine-656 (Phe-656).
E_{\text{binding}} \approx \Delta G_{\pi\text{-}\pi} (\text{Phe-656}) + \Delta G_{\text{cation-}\pi} (\text{Tyr-652}) + \Delta G_{\text{electrostatic}} (\text{Thr-623, Ser-624})

Drugs possessing a basic nitrogen (protonated at physiological pH 7.4) and aromatic rings engage in potent cation-$\pi$ interactions with Tyr-652 and multiple $\pi$-$\pi$ stacking interactions with Phe-656, literally trapping the drug molecule within the pore whenever the channel gates open.

3. Electrophysiological Cascade: From Channel Block to Torsades de Pointes

The clinical progression from hERG blockade to sudden cardiac death follows a well-defined electrophysiological sequence:

  1. $I_{Kr}$ Current Depression: Pharmacological blockage of open and inactivated $K_v11.1$ channels diminishes outward potassium repolarization current.
  2. Action Potential Duration (APD) Prolongation: Phase 3 repolarization is delayed, prolonging the effective refractory period across ventricular myocardium. On surface 12-lead ECG, this manifests as QT interval prolongation ($\text{QTc} = \frac{\text{QT}}{\sqrt{\text{RR}}} > 480\text{–}500\text{ ms}$).
  3. Early Afterdepolarizations (EADs): Delayed repolarization allows time for L-type calcium channels ($\text{Ca}_V1.2$) to recover from voltage-dependent inactivation while the membrane potential remains in their window activation zone (-30 to 0 mV). Inward calcium current reactivation produces a secondary depolarization spike (EAD) during Phase 2 or 3.
  4. Transmural Dispersion of Repolarization (TDR): Mid-myocardial (M) cells exhibit inherently longer action potentials than subepicardial or subendocardial layers. $I_{Kr}$ block exaggerates this transmural gradient, establishing a substrate for functional unidirectional block and re-entrant excitation.
  5. Torsades de Pointes (TdP): Triggered activity from an EAD occurring during the vulnerable "R-on-T" period initiates polymorphic ventricular tachycardia characterized by QRS complexes that continuously twist their electrical axis around the isoelectric baseline at rates of 180–250 bpm, frequently degenerating into Ventricular Fibrillation (VF) and hemodynamic collapse.

High-Risk Torsadogenic Drug Classes

Class III Antiarrhythmics: Amiodarone (hERG block balanced by multi-channel block), Dofetilide, Sotalol.
Antipsychotics / Neuroleptics: Haloperidol, Thioridazine, Ziprasidone.
Antimicrobials: Macrolides (Erythromycin, Azithromycin), Fluoroquinolones (Moxifloxacin), Hydroxychloroquine.
Electrolyte Exacerbators: Hypokalemia (<3.5 mEq/L) directly accelerates hERG channel inactivation and augments drug binding sensitivity; Hypomagnesemia impairs Na+/K+ ATPase and enhances EAD generation.

5. The Comprehensive In-Vitro Proarrhythmia Assay (CiPA) Paradigm

Recognizing that isolated hERG IC50 screening resulted in the false abandonment of safe cardiovascular therapeutics (such as Verapamil, which blocks hERG but simultaneously blocks inward L-type $Ca^{2+}$ current to neutralize proarrhythmia), the FDA and ICH adopted the CiPA initiative.

CiPA replaces simple hERG cutoff thresholds with multi-channel in-silico cardiomyocyte simulations: integrating experimental patch-clamp data across hERG ($I_{Kr}$), $K_v7.1/KCNE1$ ($I_{Ks}$), $K_v4.3$ ($I_{to}$), $Na_V1.5$ ($I_{Na}$, both peak and late), and $Ca_V1.2$ ($I_{Ca,L}$) into Markov state models of human ventricular action potentials to compute the qNet metric (net charge carried by repolarizing currents).
